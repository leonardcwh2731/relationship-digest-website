Case study · A Managing Partner at a PR agency

Lost the job in two days. Got it back in five weeks.

An old peer had ignored the Client’s coffee invite for 7 months. Three days after one personal note, he sent an $18K sizzle-reel brief. The job went to someone else two days after the estimate, then came back to the Client five weeks later.

The relationship

The contact and the Client worked on big events together years ago. In March 2025 the Client got back in touch, and he replied warmly: “Will absolutely keep you in mind for any video needs!” He then became a partner at a new PR agency. The Client’s notes in May, July and November 2025 went unanswered. The last one was an invitation to coffee.

What VeraOps flagged

June 15, 2026

He was third on the Client’s weekly list from VeraOps, last contacted about 7 months earlier.

Why now
“Reopening the unanswered pre-holiday meetup thread from last fall, a real specific dangling item, now that there’s a solid stretch of new work to share.”
Suggested message
“Hey [name], Been a minute since I floated that NYC coffee or Zoom before the holidays. Life moves fast. We have had a strong run on our end since then… Would love to reconnect and hear what [agency] has on the slate. Worth a quick call?”

How the conversation unfolded

  1. Jul 8 – Aug 3The Client sends a general news update and two follow-ups. No reply.
  2. Sep 8The Client sends a personal note: a current theatre project brought back memories of the awards-show work they did together. “I’d love to reconnect… Also, selfishly, I just miss working with ya.”
  3. Sep 11The brief, 3 days later: he sends a sizzle-reel request for one of his clients. “Great to be connected (HI [NAME]!!)”
  4. Sep 14Call with the contact and a colleague.
  5. Sep 15$18K estimate sent.
  6. Sep 17His client already had a friend producing it. He writes: “let’s stay in touch, we have TONS of upcoming projects… so great to be reconnected!”
  7. Oct 5–8The Client invites him for coffee. “I would LOVE to catch up.”
  8. Oct 21The brief comes back: “In an exciting plot twist, we would very much like to move forward with your proposal.”
  9. Oct 30Kick-off call with his client’s team.
  10. Nov 4–6Statement of work with his client’s legal team.

All dates 2026.

What it shows

VeraOps’ June suggestion put him back on the Client’s list. A general update got no reply, but a personal, specific note got a brief in three days. When the job briefly went elsewhere, the Client was still the studio he came back to.
