About

I spent years chasing new relationships. The best ones were already in the inbox.

Before VeraOps, I ran an outbound practice. Our job was to start conversations with people who had never heard of our clients. We got good at it. And the whole time, the relationships those founders already had were going quiet.

The people who had hired them before, worked with them on set, or moved to a new company and would have loved to hear from them were sitting in the inbox, untouched. We were working hard on strangers while the warmest relationships cooled.

The Ghost Robot story

Ghost Robot showed me what that costs. They hired someone to run business development, and the relationships that person built and looked after went with them when they left. The studio had the history and the work. The conversations had walked out the door.

That’s when it clicked for me. Relationships are the studio’s most valuable asset, and almost nobody looks after them on purpose.

What I saw in founders’ inboxes

Every studio founder I’ve worked with has the same thing: ten, fifteen, twenty years of people who trust them. Former clients who moved up. Producers who became CMOs. Collaborators who started their own agencies. The best work they’ll win next year will start with one of them.

But the work gets busy, and the conversations stop. Then projects arrive as briefs and RFPs, and the founder is one of five studios pitching on price.

Why I built this

VeraOps exists so the founder is back in those conversations, months before the brief. Our team does the work of knowing who to talk to, and why now. The founder does what only they can: have the conversation.

If that sounds like your studio, I’d like to talk.
