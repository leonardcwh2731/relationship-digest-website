01The problem

You built your studio on relationships. Then the work got busy, the conversations stopped, and new projects started arriving as briefs and RFPs, where you compete on price.

02The reframe

It’s a timing problem, not a pipeline problem.

The decision to hire a studio is made months before the brief is written, even before the public signals: a new CMO, a reorg, a product launch. It starts in private conversations.

While everyone else pitches for a project, the studios already in the conversation shape it.

03Our point of view

Stop chasing deals. Start having better conversations.

The work still comes, but because you were early, not lucky. Getting there comes down to three principles.

Stay close.

Keep in regular, human contact with the people who already trust you.

Hear early.

Learn what is changing inside their companies before it shows up anywhere public.

Move first.

Bring an idea before the brief exists, so the brief is written around you.

Case studies

Three conversations that started before the brief.

Three studio founders, three relationships that had gone quiet for months or years, and what happened once the conversation started again. Each studio is named only as “Client”.

The Client hadn’t emailed this contact in 8.5 years. Eleven days after one note, they were invited into a private, NDA-only RFP. The project went public three months later. It is now a $67K campaign.

The relationship

In 2017 she hired the Client for a product video. Their last email was that October. In March 2026 she became VP Marketing and Communications at a major science museum.

What VeraOps flagged · March 30, 2026

“Job change to a senior marketing role at a major cultural institution, prime moment to reconnect as she’s establishing new production partnerships.”

How it unfolded · 2026

  1. Apr 3The Client sends the suggested note. She replies in 23 minutes.
  2. Apr 14The brief: a private RFP for “an early-stage project… featuring a widely-known IP”. NDA required. Budget up to $100K.
  3. May 29Pitch to the museum team.
  4. Jul 7First public signal: she announces she is leading the exhibition.
  5. Oct 23Photography and retouching added. Revised to $67K.
  6. Nov 9Kick-off call.

No feed would have surfaced this project in April. The Client was on the RFP list because they reconnected within days of her new job, not after the news broke.

An old peer had ignored the Client’s coffee invite for 7 months. Three days after one personal note, he sent an $18K brief. The job went elsewhere, then came back five weeks later.

The relationship

They worked on big events together years ago. He then became a partner at a new PR agency. The Client’s notes in May, July and November 2025 went unanswered; the last was an invitation to coffee.

What VeraOps flagged · June 15, 2026

“Reopening the unanswered pre-holiday meetup thread from last fall, a real specific dangling item, now that there’s a solid stretch of new work to share.”

How it unfolded · 2026

  1. Jul–AugA general news update and two follow-ups. No reply.
  2. Sep 8A personal note: “I’d love to reconnect… selfishly, I just miss working with ya.”
  3. Sep 11The brief, 3 days later: a sizzle reel for one of his clients. $18K estimate sent on Sep 15.
  4. Sep 17His client already had a friend producing it. “Let’s stay in touch, we have TONS of upcoming projects.”
  5. Oct 21The brief comes back: “In an exciting plot twist, we would very much like to move forward with your proposal.”
  6. Nov 4–6Statement of work with his client’s legal team.

A general update got no reply; a personal, specific note got a brief in three days. When the job briefly went elsewhere, the Client was still the studio he came back to.
