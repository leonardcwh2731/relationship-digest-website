01The problem

You built your studio on relationships. Then the conversations stopped.

Three common reasons.

  1. 01

    The work takes over.

    Delivery fills the week, and reaching out is the first thing to slip.

  2. 02

    People fade from memory.

    They’re still in your inbox, but nothing brings them back to mind.

  3. 03

    Reaching out feels like chasing.

    Without a real reason to write, a note feels like pestering.

02The reframe

It’s not a timing problem. It’s a trust problem.

When you lose touch, the brief reaches you cold. You become one of five studios pitching, compared on price, compromised on margins, and asked to prove you can deliver.

And watching for the right moment won’t fix it because by the time you learn about a new hire or product launch, they could already be talking to the studios they trust.

03Our point of view

Show up consistently, with value.

Trust is built months before the brief.

  1. 01

    Stay close.

    Keep in regular, human contact with the people who already trust you.

  2. 02

    Provide value.

    Every email carries a personalized and helpful message.

  3. 03

    Be there first.

    When a need starts to form, you’re already in the conversation.

Benefits

Win work, and on better terms.

01

Low competition

Sometimes, there’s only one or two other studios involved. Sometimes, you are the only partner they think of. Either way, you skip the bidding process altogether.

02

Higher margins

The project fits what you do best, and your process is already tuned for this kind of client. You can charge more, and keep more of what you charge.

03

The upper hand

They come to you as the expert, not as one of the few studios. That puts you in the stronger position when it’s time to talk price, and makes you far more likely to win.
