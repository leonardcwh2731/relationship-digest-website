Why it works

01The problem

You built your studio on relationships. Then the conversations stopped.

The work got busy. Now projects arrive as briefs and RFPs, and you’re one of five studios, compared on price and asked to prove you can deliver.

02The reframe

It’s a timing problem, not a pipeline problem.

The decision to hire a studio is made months before the brief is written, even before the public signals: a new CMO, a reorg, a product launch. It starts in private conversations.

Once the brief is out, the client is comparing studios they don’t know. Before it, they’re talking to people they already trust.

03Our point of view

Get there first, and there’s no one to compete with.

When a project starts with you, there’s no line-up of agencies, no race to the lowest price, and no doubt about whether you can deliver. They’ve already seen you do the work. And you win it with a client who trusts you: one who doesn’t bargain over your pricing or micromanage your team.

Getting there comes down to three principles:

  • Stay close. Keep in regular, human contact with the people who already trust you.
  • Hear early. Learn what is changing inside their companies before it shows up anywhere public.
  • Move first. Bring an idea before the brief exists, so the brief is written around you.

What being early gets you

Win the work on better terms.

01

Little to no competition

There’s no RFP to bid on. The relationship is already built, so there’s rarely anyone to compete with. Even when one or two other studios are in the picture, you skip the bidding process altogether.

02

Higher margins

The project fits what you do best, and your process is already tuned for this kind of client. You can charge more, and keep more of what you charge.

03

The upper hand

They come to you as the expert, not as one of five options. That puts you in the stronger position when it’s time to talk price, and makes you far more likely to win.

How it works

A managed service, not software.

Each week we tell you who to talk to and why now, keep track of what you hear, and review your key accounts with you each quarter. One framework, built for one kind of founder.

01

Relationship strategy

We decide which accounts and which clusters of people to focus on. We surface the unique clusters in your network, like a team you once worked with at one brand, since promoted or spread across new companies, then tell you which contacts to prioritise and which accounts to pursue.

A clear plan: who to talk to, which accounts to go after, and why.

02

Relationship context

Three sources, combined into one picture of every relationship. We map your full history and find the real person behind every address, even after a job change.

  • Email inbox
  • Calendar
  • Meeting notetaker

Every reconnection starts from what was actually said.

03

Relationship signals

The moments that make it natural to reach out:

  • Job changes, since you last spoke and recently
  • Promotions and newly added roles
  • Company news
  • LinkedIn posts
  • Website visits, including people who read your note but didn’t reply

You reach out when there’s a real reason to.

04

Inbox health

We look after the inbox your notes go out from:

  • Sending policies monitored: SPF, DKIM and DMARC
  • Domain and IP reputation tracked
  • Inbox placement tests
  • Email warm-up

Your notes land in the inbox, and your name stays trusted.

05

Weekly account report

Who we picked, the context we used and how each note was written, plus why this person, why not others, why now.

A clear view of what we’re doing and why.

06

Client dashboard

Everything in the report, live: who to contact and follow up, drafted follow-ups, conversations started, and your accounts.

Every relationship in motion, in one place.

Is this for you?

Built for one kind of founder. We’re selective on purpose.

For

  • Founders of creative studios: video production, animation, design, creative and ad agencies, content and experiential studios
  • Running the studio for 10+ years, with the founder still leading sales
  • $1M+ in annual revenue, with projects of $50K+
  • A team of 5 or more, ideally up to 20
  • On Google Workspace, with relationships mostly on LinkedIn
  • Ideally serving mid-market and enterprise brands, with no recent major switch in verticals

Not for

  • Studios with a large sales team
  • Under $1M in annual revenue
  • Marketing agencies selling small offers, like SEO or paid ads
  • Serving local or brick-and-mortar businesses, like dental or home services, whose clients aren’t on LinkedIn
  • Solo operators: freelancers, contractors, coaches and consultants
  • Studios that lead with a retainer as their first offer
